# v1 — evidence + sticky 3-state HMM

`python -m src.run` → `submissions/v1.csv` (deterministic; no randomness anywhere).

## Approach

1. **Parse the raw ATD** (`src/atd.py`) into per-frame arrays in metres, cached in `cache/`.
2. **Per-frame evidence** (`src/features.py`):
   - nearest home and nearest away player to the ball, and the margin between them;
   - ball speed from consecutive sightings (0.2 s smoothing);
   - ball beyond the lines by more than 1 m;
   - mean player speed (tracker field, clipped at 12 m/s, 1 s smoothing).
3. **Emissions** (`src/model.py`), in nats per frame:
   - **team (H vs A):** `K_TEAM · exp(-d_min/3 m) · tanh(margin/2 m)`, only when the ball is seen
     inside the pitch;
   - **dead ball (D):** low mean player speed (walking pace, 1.3 m/s, is the midpoint), ball not
     seen, ball out of bounds, inside a Smart Tagging SET PIECES window. A fast-moving ball inside
     the pitch counts *against* D.
4. **Transitions** come from football priors: possessions of ~12.5 s, of which a third end in a
   stoppage, and dead-ball stretches of ~30 s.
5. **Viterbi** decoding runs per half. Ungraded frames get `X`.

### How the constants were set

Every weight comes from football reasoning or from **unlabelled** aggregate statistics of the
tracking (`notes/exploration.md`). None comes from the public ground truth. One weight, `K_TEAM`,
was then calibrated so that the decoder's *own output* has the football-typical possession
length. That uses no labels:

| K_TEAM | possessions | mean duration |
|---|---|---|
| 0.2 | 125 | 32.6 s |
| 0.4 | 187 | 22.4 s |
| 0.8 | 280 | 15.3 s |
| **1.0** | **334** | **12.9 s** |
| 1.6 | 457 | 9.5 s |

### Abstention

The brief suggested writing `X` for long ball gaps inside one state. v1 does **not** do this.
Under spellF1 the scorer fills an `X` with the previous label, which is exactly what the decoder
already outputs inside a state. Under macroF1 an `X` counts as a miss. So inside a stable state,
`X` can only lose points. It becomes worth trying once the decoder knows when it is *unsure*,
for example via posterior marginals.

## Result

- Full match: 334 possessions, mean 12.9 s. Graded frames are H 38.5%, A 29.5%, D 31.9%.
- `validate_submission.py`: OK.
- **Sanity check only**: the public 5-minute window has just 17 possessions, so this is not a
  ranking and was not used to choose anything.

| submission | macroF1 | spellF1 | poss | dur |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline_smooth125 | 0.5812 | 0.4242 | 16 | 18.5 |
| **v1** | **0.6455** | **0.3889** | 19 | 15.0 |
| baseline_smooth50 | 0.6207 | 0.3256 | 26 | 10.8 |

The repo itself notes that smooth125 wins on the public window but loses to smooth50 on the full
match. v1 beats both baselines on macroF1, and its possession count is close to the window's 17.

## What to try next

1. **Posterior-based abstention:** forward-backward marginals, with `X` only where the top state
   is uncertain *and* no hand-over is likely.
2. **Smart Tagging team priors:** phases tagged with a team (Build Up, Progression, Final Third…)
   as weak H/A evidence. The DEFENDING codes would count as evidence for the opposite team.
3. **Ball interpolation** across short gaps (< 1 s). Exploration shows almost no gaps that short,
   though: 99.9% of missing frames are in gaps over 1 s. So the long gaps need a different signal:
   team shape, meaning which team's block is advancing and the spread of player velocities.
4. **Restart detection:** players regrouping, then a sudden burst of movement. This would sharpen
   D→live boundaries, where spellF1 is most sensitive.
5. **Duration-aware model (HSMM)** to replace the geometric durations implied by a plain HMM.
6. **Broadcast feed:** only where the tactical ball is missing, and only once the two feeds are
   aligned by code.
